# Facebook mechanics research (lane fb-mech)

**Window:** current as of 2026-08-31.
**Retrieval date for living pages:** 2026-08-31.
**Decision this memo supports:** whether PLAN-0001's engage move (text-only color post plus "Comment 1 or 2" first comment) is still a safe organic mechanism for two small Icelandic Pages.
**Recency rule:** facts below are 2024 or later unless marked `BACKGROUND`.
**Nothing older than 2024 is presented as current behavior.**

## 1. Engagement bait vs a genuine question

**Answer:** Yes.
Facebook still demotes engagement bait in 2026, including "comment X" and vote-with-a-comment prompts.
The line is instructing a specific interaction (a number, YES, emoji, or reaction-as-vote), not asking a real two-sided question.

### FACTS

Meta's live Transparency Centre ranking guideline still defines **engagement bait** as posts that **explicitly request** engagement (votes, shares, comments, tags, likes or other reactions) for purposes other than a narrow set of specific calls to action (missing people or property, fundraising, petitions).
The same page carves out posts that ask people to engage in order to show they do or do not support an issue, or to share time-sensitive disaster information.
Source: Meta Transparency Centre, "Engagement bait", living page, retrieved 2026-08-31.
URL: https://transparency.meta.com/en-gb/features/approach-to-ranking/content-distribution-guidelines/engagement-bait

Meta's Business Help Center still states that engagement bait "urges people to interact with Facebook posts through likes, shares, comments and other actions to artificially boost engagement and get greater reach" and that "**Posts and Pages that use this tactic will receive less distribution**".
It names four classifier types on that page:
**React baiting** (asking people to react: like, love, care, haha, wow, sad, angry).
**Comment baiting** (asking people to comment with **specific answers: words, numbers, phrases or emojis**).
**Share baiting** (asking people to share the post).
**Vote baiting** (asking people to vote using reactions, comments, sharing, or other means of representing a vote).
This page did not render as static HTML in this session.
The quoted taxonomy is from the live Help Center text returned in search on 2026-08-31, and it matches the independent 2025 restatement below.
Source: Meta Business Help Center, "How to avoid posting engagement bait on Facebook", living page, retrieved 2026-08-31.
URL: https://www.facebook.com/business/help/259911614709806

`BACKGROUND` (policy origin, not a 2026 announcement): on 2017-12-18 Facebook announced it would **demote individual posts** that use engagement bait, then apply **stricter Page-level demotions** for Pages that do it systematically.
It explicitly spared posts that ask for help, advice, or recommendations (missing-child reports, fundraising, travel tips).
An 2018-11-13 update said Facebook would **demote comments which contain engagement bait**.
A 2019-01-30 update extended demotion to videos with bait in the audio.
The 2017 post remains the cited origin of the current Transparency Centre rule.
Source: Henry Silverman and Lin Huang, "Fighting Engagement Bait on Facebook", Meta Newsroom, published 2017-12-18, last in-page update 2019-01-30.
URL: https://about.fb.com/news/2017/12/news-feed-fyi-fighting-engagement-bait-on-facebook/

SocialRails restates Meta's five named types as **vote baiting, react baiting, comment baiting, tag baiting and share baiting**, and says the classifier "reads the *ask*, not a keyword".
It gives the operational rewrite: "tell me what you'd do" is fine; "COMMENT YES if you agree" is bait regardless of synonyms.
It also recants earlier invented penalty percentages ("up to 80%") as folklore Meta never published.
Source: SocialRails, "Facebook Algorithm Words to Avoid: What Meta Actually Demotes", published 2025-01-11.
URL: https://socialrails.com/blog/facebook-algorithm-words-to-avoid

Buffer's 2026 Facebook timing guide still lists engagement bait as a thing to avoid, with the example caption "Like this if you like dogs better, share this if you like cats better", and says Facebook "regularly updates its algorithm to downrank content like this".
Source: Kirsti Lang, "Best Time to Post on Facebook - New Data from 14M Posts", Buffer, published 2026-02-26.
URL: https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/

No Meta Newsroom post retrieved for 2024-01-01 through 2026-08-31 **repeals** the engagement-bait demotion.
The 2026 ranking work Meta did announce (feed and video ranking, original-content boost, SilverTorch retrieval) does not mention a safe harbor for "comment 1 or 2" prompts.

### INFERENCE

PLAN-0001's current first-comment copy ("Comment 1 or 2. Yarnball is taking notes.") sits **inside Meta's own comment-baiting and vote-baiting definitions**, not in the genuine-question carve-out.
The hook "Dragon or mermaid tonight?" **without an instruction to comment a specific token** is much closer to the spared class (a real two-sided question people can answer in their own words).
The line is not "any question is bait".
The line is **instructing the interaction format** (comment a number, a YES, an emoji, or a reaction-as-vote).
Moving the instruction into the first comment does **not** obviously escape the classifier: Meta has demoted bait **inside comments** since 2018, and the first comment is still a comment from the Page.
Repeated weekly use on two small Pages is the exact pattern Meta said gets **Page-level** (not just post-level) demotion.
This **does** threaten the core engage move as currently written.
It does **not** kill asking a real this-or-that question.

**Unanswerable with public evidence:** the exact 2026 false-positive rate of the classifier on Icelandic copy, and the size of the reach cut for a one-off vs a weekly habit.

## 2. Organic reach for small Pages, and whether color/background posts still work

**Answer:** Original Reels and other native video are the only formats Meta publicly boosts in 2026.
Color/background text posts still exist as a native Page format.
Preferential reach for color posts is **unanswerable** from Meta sources; there is no 2024-2026 Meta document that grants them a multiplier.
Link-in-the-body posts are treated as less native than photo, video, or text cards.
Static photo ranking vs text is **unanswerable** from Meta primary sources in this window.

### FACTS

Meta's own Q4 2025 ranking work delivered a **7% lift in views of organic Feed and video posts** on Facebook, with **video time spent growing double-digits year-over-year in the US**.
Facebook was surfacing **over 25% more same-day Reels** than in Q3 2025.
Source: Meta, "2026: AI Drives Performance", published 2026-01-28 (also the Q4 2025 earnings narrative).
URL: https://about.fb.com/news/2026/01/2026-ai-drives-performance/

On 2026-03-13 Meta said it is **prioritizing original content in Facebook Feed and Reels** and **reducing the reach of unoriginal content**.
**Views and time spent watching original Reels on Facebook approximately doubled** in H2 2025 vs H2 2024.
Original, for Pages, means content **filmed or produced directly by the Page**; remixes count only if the creator is on-screen adding something new.
Re-uploads, caption-only edits, and reaction-only stitches are deprioritized, and repeat offenders can become **non-recommendable**.
Source: Meta Newsroom, "Rewarding Original Creators on Facebook", published 2026-03-13.
URL: https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/rewarding-original-creators-on-facebook/

Q1 2026 earnings (call 2026-04-29): Facebook **total video time increased more than 8% globally** in Q1, the largest quarter-over-quarter gain in four years; US/Canada video watch time +9% from ranking improvements.
**Same-day posts represented more than 30% of recommended Reels on both Instagram and Facebook**, more than double year-ago levels.
Source: Motley Fool transcript of Meta Q1 2026 earnings call, published 2026-04-29.
URL: https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/29/meta-meta-q1-2026-earnings-call-transcript/

Q2 2026 earnings (call 2026-07-29): Facebook **video time spent increased 9% globally year-over-year** and **over 10% in the US and Canada**, attributed to ranking improvements.
Instagram Feed: **over half of recommended content is now less than one day old**.
Source: Seeking Alpha, "Meta Platforms, Inc. (META) Q2 2026 Earnings Call Transcript", published 2026-07-29.
URL: https://seekingalpha.com/article/4927296-meta-platforms-inc-meta-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript

Meta Engineering published **SilverTorch**, a unified retrieval model now "the major retrieval system behind the feed and video content people see".
It scores **multiple engagement actions (like, share, comment)** in retrieval, can pull **one to two orders of magnitude more candidates** before ranking, and streams **new items in near real time**.
The post says same-day posts now represent "a significant portion of recommendations" compared with previous systems.
Source: Lei Chen et al., "SilverTorch: Index as Model", Engineering at Meta, published 2026-05-26.
URL: https://engineering.fb.com/2026/05/26/ml-applications/silvertorch-index-as-model-new-retrieval-paradigm-recommendation-systems/

Meta Transparency Centre, "Facebook Feed Recommendations", updated 2026-06-24, describes a separate AI system that fills Feed with **public content from Pages or groups the viewer may not follow**, ranked by predicted interest and engagement.
Connected friend/Page content is a different system ("Facebook Feed").
Source: Meta Transparency Centre, living page, updated 2026-06-24, retrieved 2026-08-31.
URL: https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/fb-feed-recommendations/

Buffer's live Facebook benchmarks (last updated **2026-08-30**) from **52 million posts / 213,000 accounts / 6.9 billion engagements**:
**median reach per post = 607**;
**median engagement rate = 3.1%**;
**average posting frequency = 33 posts/month**.
Those medians mix formats and Page sizes.
They are a scale check, not a color-post study.
Source: Buffer Insights, "Facebook Benchmarks", updated 2026-08-30.
URL: https://buffer.com/insights/facebook-benchmarks

**Color / background text posts still exist as a native Page format in 2026.**
LeadConnector's Social Planner API docs, **modified 2026-05-25**, still require **no media, `textFormatPresetId`, and 130 characters or fewer**, Pages only, no groups, no link preview when a preset is applied.
Source: LeadConnector Help, "Facebook Text-Background Posts Reference", modified 2026-05-25.
URL: https://help.leadconnectorhq.com/support/solutions/articles/155000008005-facebook-text-background-posts-reference

On **2026-03-11** Piunikaweb reported an Android app bug where colored-background posts became **unreadable after tapping "See more"** (preview showed partial text; expanded view showed only the background).
Desktop and browser were fine.
Meta did not publish a confirmation.
Source: Dwayne Cubbins, Piunikaweb, published 2026-03-11.
URL: https://piunikaweb.com/2026/03/11/facebook-colored-background-text-bug-posts-unreadable-android-app/

Page-tool vendors in 2026 report an in-product Meta Professional Dashboard tip to keep **outbound URLs out of the post body** and put them in the first comment, because Feed distribution favors native posts.
That is a third-party report of in-product advice, not a Newsroom ranking paper.
Source: Kacper Kurteczka, "Put the Link in the First Comment", bundle.social, published 2026-08-04.
URL: https://bundle.social/blog/links-in-first-comment

**No Meta ranking document from 2024-2026 states that color/background text posts receive a reach multiplier.**
**No Meta ranking document from 2024-2026 states they are demoted as a format.**
**No Meta ranking document from 2024-2026 ranks static photo posts above or below short text posts.**
SEO blogs that publish format-reach tables (Reels as a percent of followers, text-only as a lower percent, and similar) do not cite Meta and contradict each other; they are not used as facts here.

### INFERENCE

For two small Pages, **Reels and other native video are the only formats Meta is actively boosting in 2026 public statements**.
Color text posts remain a **supported, native, large-in-feed card** among followers, which is a visual advantage over a grey status line, not a documented ranking bonus.
They are a **Feed format**, so they compete in the connected-content half of Feed, not in Reels discovery.
They will not, by themselves, give a 500-follower Page a 50,000-person Reel-style explosion.
A short genuine question on a color card can still earn comments from people who already follow the Page; treat that as **retention among fans**, not **acquisition**.
PLAN-0001 v1 can keep color posts for this-or-that **if** the copy is not bait, but it should not bet the brand's reach on that format alone.
Outbound links belong in a first comment when a link is required; Yarnkin and Sporbok engage posts do not need a link.
Phase 3 `type=clip` / Reels is the actual 2026 distribution lever, and those clips must be **Page-produced**, not watermarked reposts.

**Unanswerable:** current median organic reach specifically for color-background posts vs photo vs Reel on Pages under 5,000 followers.
**Unanswerable:** whether a static photo outperforms a color text card for these two Pages.

## 3. First-comment mechanics (content in the comment vs the post body)

**Answer:** Putting a **link** in the first comment can help keep the post body native.
Putting **"Comment 1 or 2"** in the first comment still looks like bait.
Author comments are ranked near the top of the thread.
No public evidence that Pages are penalized for self-commenting immediately, and no public evidence that self-comments boost Feed distribution.

### FACTS

Meta's **Facebook Feed Ranked Comments** system card, updated **2026-04-02**, says the first comments a viewer sees include "**your own comments, your friends' comments or the author's comments**", then scores remaining comments by relevance and popularity.
Source: Meta Transparency Centre, "Facebook Feed Ranked Comments", updated 2026-04-02, retrieved 2026-08-31.
URL: https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/fb-feed-ranked-comments/

Third-party Page-tool vendors in 2026 report an **in-product Meta Professional Dashboard tip** to put **outbound links in the first comment, not the caption**, because Feed distribution favors native (no-link) posts.
bundle.social documented this on **2026-08-04** and is explicit that it is **not a guarantee** and is about **links leaving Facebook**, not about hiding CTAs in general.
Source: Kacper Kurteczka, "Put the Link in the First Comment", bundle.social, published 2026-08-04.
URL: https://bundle.social/blog/links-in-first-comment

`BACKGROUND` still in force via the 2018-11-13 Newsroom update: Facebook **demotes comments which contain engagement bait**.
A first comment that says "Comment 1 or 2" is still a comment containing bait.
Source: Meta Newsroom, "Fighting Engagement Bait on Facebook", 2018-11-13 in-page update on the 2017-12-18 post.
URL: https://about.fb.com/news/2017/12/news-feed-fyi-fighting-engagement-bait-on-facebook/

No Meta Help Center, Newsroom, or Transparency Centre page retrieved in this window says Pages are **penalized for commenting on their own post immediately after publish**.
No such page says self-comments **boost post distribution**.

### INFERENCE

First comments are a **thread-visibility** tactic (author comments pin near the top of ranked comments), not a **Feed-distribution** tactic.
Putting a **URL** in the first comment is a documented, currently recommended way to keep the **post body native** when you actually need a link.
Putting **"Comment 1 or 2"** in the first comment is the opposite: it keeps bait in a surface Meta already said it demotes.
Immediate Page self-comment is ordinary Page behavior (Postiz already does it via `value[]`); there is no public evidence of a "bot-speed self-comment" penalty, but there is also no evidence it helps ranking.
If we keep a first comment, it should be **context, signature, or a link**, not an instruction to emit a token.

**Unanswerable:** whether Postiz posting the first comment milliseconds after publish is treated differently from a human Page admin typing it 20 seconds later.

## 4. Posting cadence and timing for small Pages

**Answer:** Yarnkin at four posts per week and Sporbok at two sit below the volume where 2026 studies warn of cannibalization.
Per-page timing is real as a cold-start prior and **secondary to content quality**.
Large 2026 studies agree midweek beats weekend and **disagree on the hour**.

### FACTS

Sprout Social, **updated March 2026**, analyzed nearly **2 billion engagements** across about **307,000 profiles** from **2025-11-27 to 2026-02-27**.
Facebook peaks: **Tuesdays and Wednesdays 12-8 p.m. local**; Mondays 12-1 p.m.; Thursdays 12-2 p.m. and 8 p.m.
**Best days:** Monday-Thursday.
**Worst days:** weekends (Saturday and Sunday "universally low").
Sprout's FAQ on the same article: most brands see optimal results **posting one to two times per day**; more than that "cannibaliz[es] your reach".
Source: Sprout Social, "Best Times to Post on Facebook in 2026", updated March 2026.
URL: https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-facebook/

Buffer, **2026-02-26**, **14 million Facebook posts**: single best slot **Thursday 9 a.m.** (then 8 a.m. and 10 a.m. Thursday).
Weekday **mornings 6-11 a.m.** beat afternoons 12-5 p.m.
**Best day:** Wednesday, then Thursday, then Tuesday.
**Worst day:** Saturday.
Buffer is explicit there is **no universal best time**, and that **no posting schedule fixes a post that does not resonate**.
Times are reported as locally applicable without conversion.
Source: Kirsti Lang, "Best Time to Post on Facebook - New Data from 14M Posts", Buffer, published 2026-02-26.
URL: https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/

Buffer's August 2026 benchmarks put **average** Facebook posting frequency at **33 posts/month** (about 8/week) across 213,000 accounts of all sizes.
That is a descriptive average of Buffer users, not a recommendation for a 500-follower Page.
Source: Buffer Insights, "Facebook Benchmarks", updated 2026-08-30.
URL: https://buffer.com/insights/facebook-benchmarks

Sprout and Buffer **disagree on the hour** (afternoon-evening vs morning) and **agree on midweek beating weekend**.
Crowbert's June 2026 comparison of Buffer, Sprout, Hootsuite, and SocialPilot reaches the same conclusion: studies do not agree on one hour.
Source: Crowbert, "Best Time to Post on Facebook in 2026 (4 Studies Compared)", updated June 2026.
URL: https://www.crowbert.com/best-time-to-post-on-facebook

PLAN-0001's own harvest rule (no timing Lesson with n under 20 published posts per brand/channel/type) is consistent with this disagreement.
That rule is internal to the plan, not an external Facebook fact.

### INFERENCE

Yarnkin at **four posts/week** and Sporbok at **two posts/week** sit in a **safe quality band** for small Pages.
They are well below Buffer's 33/month average and below Sprout's 1-2 posts/day brand ceiling.
Do not increase cadence to chase the Buffer average.
**Per-page timing is secondary to content quality** in every serious 2026 dataset that bothers to say so.
Global "best time" tables are a cold-start prior only.
PLAN-0001's parent windows (weekday 07:00-09:00 and 19:00-21:00, weekend 09:00-11:00 Atlantic/Reykjavik) **partially overlap** real 2026 peaks (Buffer mornings; Sprout 12-8 p.m. and Thursday 8 p.m.) but **overweight Sunday**, which both large studies call a weak day.
For Icelandic parents, **local** evening still may beat a US-weighted morning; that is a harvest question, not a global fact.

**Unanswerable from public data:** best hour for Icelandic parents of young children on Facebook in 2026.

## 5. Comment response behavior and distribution

**Answer:** A Page reply is associated with about **9.5% more reactions** on Facebook, not a proven distribution boost.
Recommended operating window: be present for the **first 1-2 hours** after publish.
That window is Buffer's judgment, not a Meta SLA.

### FACTS

Buffer data scientist Julian Winternheimer's fixed-effects study of about **1 million Facebook posts / 97,427 profiles** (posts that already had at least one comment):
posts where the creator **replied to comments** received about **9% more reactions** (cross-platform writeup 2025-11-12) / **9.5% more reactions** (Facebook-specific writeup 2026-04-08), p < 0.001.
About **54% / 53.8% of Pages** performed above their own baseline when they replied.
The metric is **reactions**, not reach or impressions, chosen to avoid circularity with comments.
Buffer states they **cannot claim causation**: strong posts may simply attract more replies.
Source: Kirsti Lang, "Replying to Comments Boosts Engagement by 5-42% on These Major Platforms", Buffer, published 2025-11-12.
URL: https://buffer.com/resources/replying-to-comments-boosts-engagement/
Source: Kirsti Lang, "Replying to Your Comments on Facebook Boosts Engagement", Buffer, published 2026-04-08.
URL: https://buffer.com/resources/replying-to-your-comments-on-facebook-boosts-engagement/

The 2026-04-08 article recommends **replying in the first couple of hours** while the post is still being distributed, and treating that as a reason to post when a human is actually available.
That timing advice is Buffer's judgment, not a Meta SLA.
Source: same Buffer article, published 2026-04-08.
URL: https://buffer.com/resources/replying-to-your-comments-on-facebook-boosts-engagement/

No Meta ranking card retrieved in this window publishes a "reply within N minutes and we will expand distribution" rule.

### INFERENCE

A Page that **answers real comments quickly** is associated with modestly more reactions.
That is **not** the same as a proven distribution multiplier, and it is the **weakest** of the six platforms Buffer measured (Threads +42%, LinkedIn +30%, Instagram +21%, Facebook +9%).
Recommended operating window for these two brands: **be present for 60-120 minutes after publish**, reply in sentences, ask a follow-up.
Do not automate "thanks!" replies.
Do not treat unanswered "1" / "2" tokens as a harvest win if the Page never talks back; those are the low-effort comments the bait classifier is built to notice.

**Unanswerable:** a numeric "boost to distribution" from fast Page replies.
Buffer did not measure reach.

## Implications for PLAN-0001

### Engage move (`social-move-engage`) - **change, do not keep as written**

The current mechanism is **comment-bait**, and Meta still demotes that in 2026, including at Page level if it is a habit.
PLAN-0001's own Facebook channel section says "Comment-bait is the engagement mechanism".
That sentence should not survive.

Keep: ask a specific two-sided question; no CTA in the first line; success is a reply.
Drop: any instruction to comment a number, a YES, an emoji, or a reaction-as-vote, in the hook **or** in the first comment.
Replace the Hopkins "keyed response" with **an answer in the commenter's own words** (dragon, mermaid, the job they actually ran today).
Harvest should count **unique substantive comments**, not token volume.

Safe rewrite of the example:
Hook: `Dragon or mermaid tonight?`
First comment, if any: flavor or signature, **not** `Comment 1 or 2`.

### This-or-that type (`social-type-this-or-that`) - **change the RULES slot, keep the shape**

Keep two named options in the hook.
Change Step 3: the first comment is **optional context**, not "tell people how to answer (1 or 2)".
If RULES stays, it cannot command a token.
Native Facebook polls remain out of scope (Postiz still does not support them); do not pretend a comment-vote is a poll.

### Facebook channel Skill (`social-channel-facebook`) - **keep color posts, add two warnings, mix formats later**

Keep: `post_type=post`, `text_format_preset_id`, empty image, 130 characters or fewer.
That format is still a live Graph/API feature as of 2026-05-25.
Add: **do not put engagement-bait copy in the post body or the first comment**.
Add: first comment is allowed for **links or signature**, and is **not** a ranking cheat.
Add: color posts are a **follower-Feed** format; they are **not** documented as a 2026 reach multiplier.
Do not claim preferential reach for backgrounds.
When Phase 3 ships `type=clip`, require **Page-original** video (Meta 2026-03-13) and treat Reels as the discovery format.
Watch Android readability of color posts after the 2026-03-11 bug; if Icelandic parents are on Android, a color card that expands to a blank field is a product defect, not a vibe.

### Cadence table - **no increase; retune Sunday; keep harvest**

No change to posts-per-week (Yarnkin 4, Sporbok 2).
Change the Sunday bedtime slot from a default to an **experiment**, because both 2026 timing studies call Sunday weak.
Keep weekday evening 19:00-21:00 as a prior for parents; add **weekday 08:00-11:00** as the Buffer prior to A/B in harvest.
Do not auto-move times until n>=20 per brand/channel/type, as PLAN-0001 already says.
Reply window: human present **the first 1-2 hours** after publish; that is Buffer's operating advice, not a Meta guarantee.

### PLAN-0001 itself - **one sentence that must go**

In the Facebook channel bullet and the "Decisions against" table, retire "Comment-bait is the engagement mechanism" / "Facebook Plugs... Comment-bait does".
Replace with: **a genuine two-option question in a native color post, optional non-bait first comment, Page replies in the first hour; Reels later for reach**.

### What this research does not license

It does not license abandoning Facebook for these two brands.
It does not license daily posting.
It does not license buying comments or pods (inauthentic engagement is a separate, still-enforced abuse class).
It does not license treating Buffer's 9.5% reaction lift as a distribution KPI.
