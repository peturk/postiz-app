# Iceland audience and language research

Research is current through 2026-08-31.
The primary evidence window is the last 90 days, with older material labeled as background or as outside the window.
Each claim is labeled **FACT**, **BACKGROUND**, **EVIDENCE GAP**, or **INFERENCE**.
Page-feed observations use public mirrors because direct Facebook feeds did not consistently expose posts or engagement data without login.
This report gives an operational reading of the new language rule, not legal advice.

## 1. Language on Icelandic consumer and B2B Pages

### Facts and background

- **FACT, 2026-05-07:** Article 14 of Iceland's Marketing Act no. 44/2026 says advertisements must be in Icelandic, with an exception for an advertisement exclusively in another language when it is directed only at foreign consumers, while article 42 says the Act took effect immediately.
  Source: [Alþingi, Markaðssetningarlög, articles 14 and 42](https://www.althingi.is/altext/157/s/1121.html), 2026-05-07.
- **FACT, 2026-05-07:** The same Act defines an advertisement broadly as a description in any form connected with commerce, business, a trade, or professional work that is intended to draw attention to a product or service.
  Source: [Alþingi, Markaðssetningarlög, article 2](https://www.althingi.is/altext/157/s/1121.html), 2026-05-07.
- **FACT, posts dated 2026-05-06 to 2026-06-11:** The observed Bókabúð Forlagsins posts were in Icelandic and tied books to Icelandic authors, cultural moments, summer reading, and a book-of-the-month rhythm.
  Source: [Glóbuya public mirror of Bókabúð Forlagsins](https://www.globuya.com/IS/Reykjav%C3%ADk/143279139149089/B%C3%B3kab%C3%BA%C3%B0-Forlagsins), posts dated 2026-05-06 through 2026-06-11, retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **BACKGROUND, posts dated 2026-02-04 to 2026-02-27:** The observed A4 húsgögn B2B posts were in Icelandic and described named office-furniture products, configurations, meeting uses, showroom visits, and an exact price offer.
  Source: [FindGlocal public mirror of A4 húsgögn](https://www.findglocal.com/IS/Reykjav%C3%ADk/103379655234967/A4-h%C3%BAsg%C3%B6gn), posts dated 2026-02-04 through 2026-02-27, retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **FACT, posts dated 2026-06-10 to 2026-07-31:** The observed Verkfæralagerinn posts were in Icelandic and connected products or opening hours to specific summer tasks, holidays, lawn care, and camping.
  Source: [FindGlocal public mirror of Verkfæralagerinn](https://www.findglocal.com/IS/K%C3%B3pavogur/102995046410424/Verkf%C3%A6ralagerinn), posts dated 2026-06-10 through 2026-07-31, retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **BACKGROUND, posts dated 2026-02-15 to 2026-04-04:** Team Spark put a complete Icelandic version first and then a complete English version in the same post, separated by slashes.
  Source: [FindGlocal public mirror of Team Spark - Formula Student Iceland](https://www.findglocal.com/IS/Unknown/159553408041/Team-Spark---Formula-Student-Iceland), posts dated 2026-02-15 through 2026-04-04, retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **FACT, undated living case:** Icelandair localized its global campaign into several market languages, while its Iceland-only campaign used the Icelandic title `Hvernig verður þitt sumar?` and a distinct local concept.
  Source: [Playable, Icelandair case](https://playable.com/cases/icelandair/), retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **EVIDENCE GAP, review completed 2026-08-31:** None of the cited current examples used an Icelandic post with the English translation relegated to a comment.
- **EVIDENCE GAP, review completed 2026-08-31:** The reviewed public sources do not establish that translation comments are a common or effective Icelandic brand convention.

### Inference and recommendation

- **INFERENCE:** English-only Facebook drafts are the wrong default for both brands when the intended audience is in Iceland.
- **INFERENCE, Yarnkin:** Publish natural Icelandic copy for the Icelandic parent audience, including the hook, body, call to action, image text, and any appended signature.
- **INFERENCE, Sporbók:** Publish natural Icelandic copy for Icelandic operators and dispatchers, using field-service vocabulary that sounds written for the work rather than translated from software marketing.
- **INFERENCE:** The Act contains no obvious language exemption for a domestic B2B campaign in the text reviewed, so Sporbók should not rely on one without Icelandic legal review.
- **INFERENCE:** A domestic organic brand post that draws attention to a product or service is likely to fall within the Act's broad advertising definition, but counsel should confirm edge cases.
- **INFERENCE:** For a genuinely mixed audience, place a short labeled English version after the Icelandic version in the same post only when both audiences need the same announcement.
- **INFERENCE:** Team Spark's international competition context explains why its same-post bilingual pattern fits that Page better than it fits a domestic consumer or operator Page.
- **INFERENCE:** For future international expansion, create separate market-specific posts or campaigns rather than making every Icelandic post bilingual.
- **INFERENCE:** Do not use the first comment as the routine translation layer because it hides essential copy, fragments moderation, and is not supported by the observed local examples.

## 2. Facebook use, relevant demographics, and Groups in Iceland in 2026

### Facts and background

- **FACT, 2026-05-29:** Gallup described Facebook as Iceland's largest social platform and reported that more than nine in ten respondents used it weekly or more often.
  Source: [Gallup, Samfélag á samfélagsmiðlum](https://www.gallup.is/frettir/samfelag-a-samfelagsmidlum/), 2026-05-29.
- **FACT, 2026-05-29:** Gallup reported that older people were more likely to use Facebook, while younger people were more likely to use TikTok.
  Source: [Gallup, Samfélag á samfélagsmiðlum](https://www.gallup.is/frettir/samfelag-a-samfelagsmidlum/), 2026-05-29.
- **FACT, 2026-05-29:** Gallup said the measurement came from its Consumption and Lifestyle survey and that detailed target-group analysis requires a paid subscription.
  Source: [Gallup, Samfélag á samfélagsmiðlum](https://www.gallup.is/frettir/samfelag-a-samfelagsmidlum/), 2026-05-29.
- **FACT, 2026-07:** NapoleonCat estimated 313,300 addressable Facebook accounts in Iceland, equal to 85.4 percent of the population, with women at 52.6 percent and men at 47.4 percent.
  Source: [NapoleonCat, Facebook users in Iceland](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/facebook-users-in-iceland/), July 2026.
- **FACT, 2026-07:** NapoleonCat estimated that ages 25-34 formed 24.3 percent and ages 35-44 formed 17.8 percent of the addressable Facebook audience.
  Source: [NapoleonCat, Facebook users in Iceland](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/facebook-users-in-iceland/), July 2026.
- **FACT, derived from the July 2026 source:** Those two available age bands total 42.1 percent, or approximately 131,900 of the reported addressable accounts.
  Source: calculation from [NapoleonCat, Facebook users in Iceland](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/facebook-users-in-iceland/), July 2026.
- **FACT, 2026-07:** NapoleonCat states that its figures come from platform marketing APIs and are estimates of addressable accounts rather than verified unique people.
  Source: [NapoleonCat, Facebook users in Iceland](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/facebook-users-in-iceland/), July 2026.
- **BACKGROUND, data from 2025-10 and report published 2025-11-08:** DataReportal reported a lower Meta planning estimate of 238,000 Facebook users, equal to 76.0 percent of Icelandic adults, and warned that ad-reach estimates are not active-user counts.
  Source: [DataReportal, Digital 2026: Iceland](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-iceland), 2025-11-08, using October 2025 data.
- **BACKGROUND, data from 2025-10 and report published 2025-11-08:** DataReportal also warned that revisions in Meta's planning tools can make apparent changes unsuitable as evidence of real audience growth or decline.
  Source: [DataReportal, Digital 2026: Iceland](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-iceland), 2025-11-08, using October 2025 data.
- **FACT, undated living page:** Gallup reports that most advertisers in its study of Iceland's largest advertisers used Facebook, that 62 percent managed foreign social and network marketing entirely themselves, and that smaller companies in the study were more likely to self-manage.
  Source: [Gallup, Samfélagsmiðlar](https://www.gallup.is/smidlar/), retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **FACT, 2026-07-30:** The Facebook Group `Mæðra tips!` had nearly 40,000 women and received more than 2,000 posts during July, often followed by many comments.
  Source: [Vísir, Sætir morðhótunum og óhróðri í vinsælum Facebook-hópi](https://www.visir.is/g/20262912834d/saetir-mordhotunum-og-ohrodri-i-vinsaelum-facebook-hopi), 2026-07-30.
- **FACT, 2026-07-30:** The same report described severe moderation pressure, hostile comments, and threats against the sole administrator of `Mæðra tips!`.
  Source: [Vísir, Sætir morðhótunum og óhróðri í vinsælum Facebook-hópi](https://www.visir.is/g/20262912834d/saetir-mordhotunum-og-ohrodri-i-vinsaelum-facebook-hopi), 2026-07-30.
- **FACT, undated living page:** Iðnaðarmenn Íslands says its Facebook Group began in 2017 to connect people with qualified tradespeople, supports more than 50 protected trades, and lets users submit work requests to verified professionals.
  Source: [Iðnaðarmenn Íslands, Um okkur](https://www.idnadarmennislands.is/um-okkur), retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **FACT, undated living page:** Iðnaðarmenn Íslands currently says that about 500 qualified tradespeople are registered with its service.
  Source: [Iðnaðarmenn Íslands, home page](https://www.idnadarmennislands.is/), retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **FACT, undated living page:** Public competitor data can show posts, reactions, comments, and shares, but reach, impressions, audience demographics, and video views remain unavailable for a Page that the researcher does not administer.
  Source: [NapoleonCat, Facebook Page Insights for Other Pages](https://napoleoncat.com/uses/facebook-page-insights-for-other-pages/), retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **EVIDENCE GAP, review completed 2026-08-31:** No current public Icelandic dataset reviewed here cross-tabulates Facebook use by parental status, trade occupation, or SMB ownership.
- **EVIDENCE GAP, review completed 2026-08-31:** No current local study reviewed here directly compares organic Page reach with Group reach for the same Icelandic audience and content.

### Inference and recommendation

- **INFERENCE:** The 25-44 bands are a useful age proxy for Yarnkin's parent audience, but they must not be described as 131,900 parents because the source does not identify parental status.
- **INFERENCE:** Facebook offers broad adult coverage for Yarnkin and is especially suitable for reaching parents rather than children.
- **INFERENCE:** The evidence for Sporbók is functional rather than demographic because the trade Group and advertiser survey show work and marketing activity without measuring Facebook use by occupation.
- **INFERENCE:** Parenting and trade Groups are important high-intent conversation spaces, but the evidence does not justify the stronger claim that Groups produce more reach than Pages.
- **INFERENCE:** Keep the Page as the controlled home for publishing, service, advertising, and first-party measurement.
- **INFERENCE:** Use relevant Groups for listening, learning vocabulary, and administrator-approved participation rather than covert promotion.
- **INFERENCE:** Yarnkin should treat parenting Groups as sensitive communities and avoid seeding posts, harvesting stories, or turning vulnerable discussions into content.
- **INFERENCE:** Sporbók should contribute only concrete operational help in trade communities and should never frame worker visibility as surveillance.
- **INFERENCE:** Do not average the NapoleonCat and DataReportal totals because their dates, methods, and definitions differ.

## 3. Concrete Icelandic Page exemplars

### Evidence boundary

- **FACT, undated living page:** Meta does not expose reach, impressions, audience demographics, or video views for a Page that the researcher does not administer.
  Source: [NapoleonCat, Facebook Page Insights for Other Pages](https://napoleoncat.com/uses/facebook-page-insights-for-other-pages/), retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **EVIDENCE GAP, review completed 2026-08-31:** The public mirrors exposed post text and dates but did not provide stable, complete reaction and comment totals.
- **INFERENCE:** The examples below are observable language, cadence, and interaction-pattern exemplars rather than a ranked list of Page engagement winners.
- **INFERENCE:** Any statement about a likely comment trigger is identified as inference unless a source reports actual discussion.

### Exemplar 1: Bókabúð Forlagsins

- **FACT, posts dated 2026-05-06 to 2026-06-11:** Five visible posts over roughly five weeks featured a children's book, an Icelandic musician, new editions of Halldór Laxness, a summer-book week, and a monthly selection.
  Source: [Glóbuya public mirror of Bókabúð Forlagsins](https://www.globuya.com/IS/Reykjav%C3%ADk/143279139149089/B%C3%B3kab%C3%BA%C3%B0-Forlagsins), posts dated 2026-05-06 through 2026-06-11, retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **FACT, posts dated 2026-05-06 to 2026-06-11:** The visible copy was Icelandic, specific to the book or cultural moment, and usually short enough to reach the offer quickly.
  Source: [Glóbuya public mirror of Bókabúð Forlagsins](https://www.globuya.com/IS/Reykjav%C3%ADk/143279139149089/B%C3%B3kab%C3%BA%C3%B0-Forlagsins), posts dated 2026-05-06 through 2026-06-11, retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **INFERENCE:** This is the closest content analogue for Yarnkin because it sells a story or reading occasion through cultural and seasonal relevance rather than talking down to children.
- **EVIDENCE GAP:** The mirror does not expose enough comment data to call a specific post a comment winner.

### Exemplar 2: A4 verslanir and A4 húsgögn

- **BACKGROUND, 2026-01-29 and 2026-02-26:** A4 verslanir mixed practical notices with a yarn giveaway and a timed puzzle that explicitly asked people to say how it went.
  Source: [FindGlocal public mirror of A4 verslanir](https://www.findglocal.com/IS/Egilssta%C3%B0ir/103941172102418/A4-verslanir), posts dated 2026-01-29 and 2026-02-26, retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **BACKGROUND, posts dated 2026-02-04 to 2026-02-27:** A4 húsgögn published several times per week in the observed period and led with a named object, a concrete feature, or a work use case.
  Source: [FindGlocal public mirror of A4 húsgögn](https://www.findglocal.com/IS/Reykjav%C3%ADk/103379655234967/A4-h%C3%BAsg%C3%B6gn), posts dated 2026-02-04 through 2026-02-27, retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **INFERENCE:** The puzzle plus a direct invitation to report the result is the clearest Page-comment mechanism in the observed sample.
- **INFERENCE:** A4 húsgögn is useful for Sporbók because it shows that local B2B copy can be Icelandic, concrete, and lightly human without becoming corporate English.

### Exemplar 3: Verkfæralagerinn

- **FACT, posts dated 2026-06-10 to 2026-07-31:** The visible Page posted approximately weekly to fortnightly, with additional opening-hours notices around holidays.
  Source: [FindGlocal public mirror of Verkfæralagerinn](https://www.findglocal.com/IS/K%C3%B3pavogur/102995046410424/Verkf%C3%A6ralagerinn), posts dated 2026-06-10 through 2026-07-31, retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **FACT, posts dated 2026-06-10 to 2026-07-31:** Posts connected products to immediate jobs such as preparing a summer gathering, packing for camping, or improving a lawn.
  Source: [FindGlocal public mirror of Verkfæralagerinn](https://www.findglocal.com/IS/K%C3%B3pavogur/102995046410424/Verkf%C3%A6ralagerinn), posts dated 2026-06-10 through 2026-07-31, retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **INFERENCE:** This Page is a strong local-services model because season and weather change what the customer should do rather than serving as generic Iceland decoration.
- **EVIDENCE GAP:** The mirror does not expose enough comment data to compare these posts by discussion volume.

### Exemplar 4: Icelandair

- **FACT, undated living case:** Icelandair's Iceland-only quiz recorded more than 8,200 sessions, more than 5,700 registrations, a 73 percent completion rate, and more than 6,300 website clicks.
  Source: [Playable, Icelandair case](https://playable.com/cases/icelandair/), retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **FACT, undated living case:** Its separate global game was localized for multiple countries and promoted through Facebook and Instagram organically and through paid media, newsletters, and an outdoor placement.
  Source: [Playable, Icelandair case](https://playable.com/cases/icelandair/), retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **INFERENCE:** The useful pattern is separate local and global creative, not automatic bilingual duplication on one Icelandic Page.
- **EVIDENCE GAP:** The campaign totals are vendor-reported and multi-channel, so they cannot be attributed to Facebook Page posts alone.

### Exemplar 5: Blush

- **FACT, undated living case:** Blush's drop game recorded more than 34,600 sessions without paid media and was promoted through organic Facebook and Instagram posts plus email.
  Source: [Playable, Blush case](https://playable.com/cases/blush/), retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **FACT, undated living case:** The case reports that players discussed strategies in Facebook Groups, giving direct evidence that a useful or competitive brand activity can move into peer conversation.
  Source: [Playable, Blush case](https://playable.com/cases/blush/), retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **INFERENCE:** Participation, a reason to return, and a result worth comparing created the discussion mechanism, while a plain product claim would not provide those same social objects.
- **EVIDENCE GAP:** The figures are vendor-reported and multi-channel, and the source does not isolate Page comments from Group discussion or email traffic.

### Cross-example conclusion

- **INFERENCE:** The repeatable local pattern is Icelandic copy plus one concrete object, task, occasion, question, or playable action.
- **INFERENCE:** Direct offers are present, but the stronger examples give the audience something useful, culturally timely, or participatory before asking for a click.
- **INFERENCE:** The observed cadence ranges from roughly weekly to several times per week, so it does not validate a universal best cadence for either brand.
- **INFERENCE:** Keep the planned Yarnkin and Sporbók cadences as test priors and judge them from each Page's own reach, meaningful comments, clicks, and negative-feedback data.

## 4. Cultural tone on Icelandic Facebook

### Facts and background

- **BACKGROUND, 2024-11-30:** A peer-reviewed study describes Facebook as an important communication space for Icelanders and characterizes Icelandic Facebook writing as informal, sometimes in tension with formal language ideology.
  Source: [IRIS, Icelandic on Facebook: Linguistic practices in an informal communication space](https://iris.rais.is/en/publications/icelandic-on-facebook-linguistic-practices-in-an-informal-communi), published 2024-11-30.
- **FACT, posts sampled from 2026-01-29 to 2026-07-31:** The Page sample repeatedly uses direct second-person Icelandic, short practical statements, local dates, named products, and specific seasonal occasions.
  Sources: [A4 verslanir](https://www.findglocal.com/IS/Egilssta%C3%B0ir/103941172102418/A4-verslanir), [Bókabúð Forlagsins](https://www.globuya.com/IS/Reykjav%C3%ADk/143279139149089/B%C3%B3kab%C3%BA%C3%B0-Forlagsins), and [Verkfæralagerinn](https://www.findglocal.com/IS/K%C3%B3pavogur/102995046410424/Verkf%C3%A6ralagerinn), posts dated 2026-01-29 through 2026-07-31, retrieved 2026-08-31.
- **FACT, 2026-05-07:** Marketing directed at people under 18 may not exploit guilt, social insecurity, low self-esteem, or appearance pressure under article 15 of the Marketing Act.
  Source: [Alþingi, Markaðssetningarlög, article 15](https://www.althingi.is/altext/157/s/1121.html), 2026-05-07.
- **EVIDENCE GAP, review completed 2026-08-31:** No current representative study reviewed here measures Icelanders' tolerance for hard-sell Facebook copy, emoji volume, or brand humor.

### Inference and recommendation

- **INFERENCE, authentic:** Use conversational but correctly edited Icelandic, direct second person, concrete nouns, and one recognizable local detail when it changes the story or task.
- **INFERENCE, annoying:** Avoid translated English slogans, corporate abstraction, generic stock emotion, artificial urgency, stacked exclamation marks, and tourist shorthand for Iceland.
- **INFERENCE, humor:** Do not encode a national humor norm from this sample, and test restrained situational humor only when it grows from the product or task.
- **INFERENCE, formality:** Sound like a capable local person speaking to another adult rather than a government notice, a US growth marketer, or a child entertainer.
- **INFERENCE, weather:** Mention darkness, rain, wind, snow, or summer only when it changes bedtime, travel, opening hours, equipment, scheduling, or field work.
- **INFERENCE, hard sell:** One clear offer or deadline is acceptable when it is factual and relevant, but usefulness or participation should carry the post rather than pressure.
- **INFERENCE, Yarnkin dignity:** Speak to the parent and never imply that a good parent must buy, that a child is falling behind, or that the product guarantees sleep, learning, calm, or family closeness.
- **INFERENCE, Sporbók dignity:** Describe the job, handoff, schedule, or constraint without belittling paper processes, office staff, technicians, or crews.
- **INFERENCE:** A native Icelandic editorial pass is a quality gate because grammatical Icelandic can still sound translated or commercially unnatural.

- **INFERENCE:** Facebook remains a justified first channel in Iceland, but the strategy should be based on first-party Page results rather than unstable third-party audience totals.

## Implications for the brand Skills

All items below are recommendations derived from the evidence and inferences above.

### `social-brand-yarnkin`

- Add an Iceland-market rule that the hook, body, call to action, image text, and signature must be written in natural Icelandic for the Icelandic Facebook Page.
- Keep the existing rule that the account speaks to parents rather than children.
- Add explicit prohibitions on parental guilt, child inadequacy, developmental claims, sleep guarantees, and pressure framed as family care.
- Define the useful tone as warm, quiet, conversational Icelandic with one concrete bedtime choice, ritual, story detail, or gentle parent question.
- State that Reykjavik is a place of origin and lived texture, not a cue for aurora, puffin, waterfall, or souvenir imagery.
- Do not append the current English signature `Yarnkin · bedtime stories from Reykjavik` to Iceland-targeted posts.
- Omit the signature until a native editor and legal reviewer approve an Icelandic variant, while retaining the exact English signature for a future English-market campaign.

### `social-brand-sporbok`

- Add an Iceland-market rule that the hook, body, call to action, image text, and signature must be written in natural Icelandic for the Icelandic Facebook Page.
- Require verified field-service vocabulary and concrete work objects, constraints, handoffs, and outcomes rather than translated software abstractions.
- Clarify that weather belongs only when it changes the job, route, device use, signal, schedule, safety, or customer communication.
- Add prohibitions on surveillance framing, worker scoring, belittling paper or phone workflows, and implying that dispatchers or technicians create chaos.
- Allow restrained situational humor only when it grows from a real task and leaves the operator's competence intact.
- Do not append the current English signature `Sporbok · field work, without the chaos` to Iceland-targeted posts.
- Omit the signature until a native editor and legal reviewer approve an Icelandic variant, while retaining the exact English signature for a future English-market campaign.

### Shared Facebook implementation

- Put the enforceable domestic-language, bilingual-routing, translation-placement, and legal-review rules in the Facebook channel Skill so the rule has one operational home.
- Use separate localized posts or campaigns for separate markets.
- Permit an English block after the Icelandic block only for a genuinely mixed audience and only after the essential Icelandic version is complete.
- Do not make the first comment the standard English translation location.
- Require a native Icelandic editorial pass before approval.
- Treat cadence as an experiment and evaluate at least 20 comparable posts per brand before changing the schedule on engagement evidence.
- Measure each Page with its own reach, meaningful comments, link clicks, hides, unfollows, and moderation load rather than competitor reach estimates.
