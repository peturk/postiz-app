02

Áhöfnin: four objects, one rig

Built exactly like the Yarnkin animals: rounded rectangles, circles and rounded triangles, flat fill, a round eye that never changes shape, lids that carry the feeling. Unlike Yarnkin there are no mouths and no limbs. Adults read competence from eyes and posture, and a mouth is what turns an object into a toy. Try each state.

How they behave

  • A state is a pool of expressions on a random cadence, never one held face.
  • They react to the user's own events: a receipt logged, a job closed, the day ended.
  • The pointer has to come close and move to earn a look. Then the eyes go back to their own business.
  • Off duty, every eye closes. Nothing watches after hours.
  • Reduced motion: the pose still shows the state, nothing moves.

Where they appear

  • Social: about one post in three or four, one character per post, the full cast only for milestones.
  • Product: empty states, onboarding, a finished job, the off-duty screen.
  • Marketing: the cover, the share card, the landing page's day story.
  • Real photos of real sites stay the trust layer. The cast supports, it never replaces them.

Never

  • Mouths, teeth, arms, hands, thumbs-up, winks or blush.
  • Speech bubbles or sales copy. The cast does not speak; that is Oddur's job.
  • Watching, counting or timing a person. Hjálmurinn looks after the wearer, never at them.
  • Money, invoice errors, payment or legal screens.
  • Confetti, bouncing, or multi-wobble springs.
  • Kassinn in a lowered-lid stare. Eyes in a dark slot under a heavy lid is someone hiding in a box, watching.
  • AI-generated character art. Stills come from the rig, never from an image model.

05

Tagline

Written from the voice guide, not translated. One of these replaces the three taglines in use today.

Þú vinnur verkið. Sporbók man söguna.

RecommendedThe voice guide's own line. It carries the name: the bók keeps the saga. It speaks to the worker, not the office.

Vinnudagbók sem skrifar sig sjálf.

ClearestSays exactly what the product is. Best as the subline under the name.

Lokaðu kassanum. Reikningsdrögin eru tilbúin.

Most concreteAn outcome at a moment everyone knows, and Kassinn acts it out. Strong for ads.

Á vakt á verkstað. Frítíminn er þinn.

Privacy pillarAnswers the fear first. Right for the privacy campaign; too defensive as the main line.

Hver mínúta skilar sér.

ShortestFair pay in four words. Loses the log idea.

Frá mætingu til uppgjörs.

Current siteAccurate but abstract. Describes the process, not what the worker gets back.

Rejected: "Hvert spor skráð." A good pun on the name, but "every step recorded" is exactly the tracking fear the voice guide bans.

06

Templates, drawn by the rig

Every character here is the same code as the live cast, frozen in a chosen expression and aimed at the headline. The export script renders these exact frames to PNG at full size for Postiz. Copy is real Icelandic, written to the social Skills: no bait, no link in the body, no signature line.

07

From proposal to shipped

Once you pick a typeface and a tagline and approve the cast, the rest is mechanical.

  1. YouPick the typeface and the tagline, and approve Vinnugalli and Áhöfnin.Everything below waits on this.
  2. available (sporbok.is)Land the kit in the Sporbók repo.Colour tokens replace the Stripe indigo; the cast's art data and a pose library of PNGs go under /brand/cast/; the brand README gets the cast rules; the share card, aurora and whale images, and Söhne go.
  3. @outofcow/creaturesOne rig for everyone.Move the Yarnkin rig into the package as a data-driven Mascot API next to Oddur's engine, so Yarnkin and Sporbók art are data in their own repos and nobody forks the engine again. Needs its own plan and a major version.
  4. OutOfCow media stepRender posts from the rig.The social Flow picks a template, a character and a mood; the exporter renders the PNG at full size and uploads it to Postiz. No image model touches the characters.
  5. postiz-app SkillsTeach the social Skills the cast.Frequency, one character per post, the never-list, and the off-duty beat for Friday afternoons.
